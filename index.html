[THE_CORE_FRICTION]

Five points where the law and the model collide.

Each issue is a live dispute, not a settled fact.

01
[ISSUE]

Training Without Consent

Generative models are built on billions of copyrighted images scraped from the web — almost never with the artist's permission. The act of learning from a work, the argument goes, is not the same as copying it.

02
[ISSUE]

The 'Fair Use' Fog

Whether AI training counts as transformative 'fair use' or large-scale infringement remains unsettled. Courts are deciding it case by case, leaving creators in legal limbo.

03
[ISSUE]

Income & Attribution Erosion

AI outputs compete directly with the human work they were trained on — often without credit or compensation. Illustrators report lost commissions and declining rates.

04
[ISSUE]

The Authorship Question

If a model generates an image, who is the author — the prompter, the developer, the artists in the training set, or no one at all? Copyright law was never written for this.

05
[ISSUE]

Proof Is Hard

Models rarely reproduce a source image exactly, so proving your work was used is technically difficult. Detection tools and dataset audits are still catching up to the scale of scraping.
